# Ankit Kapoor — AI Strategy Portfolio
## Business requirements, art direction, and coding-agent handoff

Owner: Ankit Kapoor | Version: 1.0 | Date: 6 September 2026

Status: implementation specification. Build a personal portfolio around three independent projects: Disrupt This Business, The Moat Test, and Priced In. Project concepts are agreed; implementation, research, and measured results must be labeled according to their actual state. This document is standalone and does not authorize inventing personal details or deploying publicly without the host environment's required controls.

## 1. Business purpose and positioning

Create a memorable, credible personal website that helps an interviewer understand how Ankit connects AI technology to competitive strategy, customer value, and business economics. The website should feel like a carefully edited personal research publication with working experiments.

Positioning statement: “I investigate how AI changes competition, customer value, and business economics.” Supporting line: “Former machine learning engineer. MBA candidate at USC Marshall.” These statements reflect the supplied context. Do not imply that Ankit has already held an AI strategist title, advised named clients, or delivered the projected outcomes in these projects.

Primary conversion: a relevant visitor opens a project and understands Ankit's recommendation and reasoning. Secondary conversion: a visitor launches a working demo, opens a resume when supplied, or uses a verified contact link. Avoid optimizing for vanity impressions or forcing a lead form.

## 2. Audience and reading modes

Recruiter mode: within approximately 20 seconds, understand name, background, target field, and the three bodies of work. Interviewer mode: within two minutes, inspect the question, recommendation, evidence, trade-off, and uncertainty in one case. Deep-reader mode: inspect methodology and operate the demo. These are design goals to test with people, not claims about guaranteed attention.

The home page supplies fast orientation; project pages supply depth; demos supply interaction. Visitors should never have to play a game or run a model just to discover the point of a project. All important case content remains readable without JavaScript.

## 3. Scope and release plan

P0: responsive homepage; three distinct case-study pages; About page; methodology page; configurable contact/resume links; project-status handling; real or accurately labeled preview assets; metadata, social previews, sitemap, 404 page, accessible navigation, and content schema. The site works as a static publication without any API keys.

P1: essays/notes once real writing exists, privacy-respecting analytics if configured, and public hosting/custom domain under the user's deployment instructions. P2: a content-management system only if repository-based editing becomes cumbersome.

Excluded: visitor accounts, a chat-with-my-resume bot, newsletter popups, simulated client testimonials, invented impact statistics, an empty blog, autogenerated research articles, skill percentage bars, and a full-screen loading animation. Project applications remain independently runnable and are linked through configuration; the portfolio does not need to embed their servers.

## 4. Information architecture and routing

Required routes: /, /work/disrupt-this-business, /work/the-moat-test, /work/priced-in, /about, /method, and /404. Navigation: Work, About, Contact. Work links to /#work from the homepage and the absolute home anchor from other routes. Contact is a footer section linked by /#contact unless a verified external link is configured. Method is a quiet footer link, not a competing primary navigation item.

No duplicate /projects and /work taxonomies. Each project URL is stable and has a unique page title, canonical URL after domain configuration, and social preview. Demo URLs live in content, not hard-coded UI components. Open an internal demo in the same tab; an external application may open a clearly indicated new tab with safe rel attributes.

## 5. Homepage composition

Header: Ankit Kapoor as a plain typographic wordmark on the left; three navigation items on the right. Height approximately 80 px on desktop and 64 px on mobile. No AK badge, generic AI logo, or decorative avatar is required. Keep navigation visible without a sticky element obscuring headings or keyboard focus.

Hero: a large left-aligned statement occupies eight of twelve grid columns. Use the positioning statement from section 1, with line breaks chosen at each breakpoint rather than a single heading awkwardly shrinking to fit. Below: the background line, two concise sentences about the portfolio, and View the work as the primary action. A quieter About me link is secondary.

The four-column companion is an “Index of questions” containing three linked prompts: How does AI change competition? What remains worth paying for? What do the economics require? This is text with small project numbers, not three rounded cards. On mobile it follows the introduction and can be omitted from the smallest first viewport while remaining later in the document.

Selected work: three full-width features separated by thin rules. Each has a project number, title, specific strategic question, 50–80-word introduction, status, and a large evidence preview. Alternate a 5/7 and 7/5 text/image relationship on desktop to establish rhythm. Mobile always shows title, question, preview, summary, and actions in that order. Do not convert this section into a uniform three-card grid.

Project 01, Disrupt This Business: show a decision scene with two competing positions and a short real or illustrative cash/customer trace. CTA Read the case; secondary Play the scenario only when a working demo exists. Project 02, The Moat Test: show two output excerpts with evidence line references and an explicit data-mode label. Project 03, Priced In: show an expectations contour from an actual sample model or a labeled schematic until implemented.

Following the work, include a brief “How I approach a question” section: investigate the mechanism, test the assumptions, make the trade-off explicit. Give one concrete sentence per step. End with a short About preview and restrained contact section. Footer includes name, location if desired, verified links, and an automatically current year. Avoid repeating all header links and a second oversized CTA.

## 6. Project case-study template

Every case begins with a project number, question, title, one-sentence purpose, actual status, and an appropriate primary action. A 30-second brief then presents Decision, Position, Evidence, Trade-off, and Uncertainty. If a conclusion is not yet supported, Position says “Investigation in progress” and explains the proposed test; it must not invent a final recommendation.

Long-form body sections: context and decision; alternatives considered; method and model; evidence/results; interpretation; limitations; next test; sources. Each project may vary the pacing and visual treatment while retaining these semantic roles. Use a sticky contents rail on wide screens only if the article is long enough to warrant it.

Include a visible authorship block: Ankit's contribution, AI-assisted implementation, and human review status. This is where the portfolio shows the thinking behind the software. Cite precise evidence records and label illustrative or synthetic content. Demonstrate trade-offs through actual examples rather than a generic list of capabilities.

Downloads appear only when files exist. Display real file type and size, with descriptive link text. A demo outage must leave the case readable and offer View the walkthrough or sample only if that asset exists. Do not create dead buttons, fake terminal recordings, or pretend screenshots of completed products.

The bio may connect technical experience, business study, curiosity, and a preference for testing assumptions. Employer names, exact dates, job titles beyond those confirmed, thesis titles, award claims, and impact numbers require user-provided verification before inclusion.

Optional personal paragraph may mention writing and an interest in film/music, but keep the homepage focused on work. Do not use private financial, health, residential, immigration, or family details from conversation context. Do not invent an email address, LinkedIn slug, GitHub URL, headshot, resume, or custom domain.

Missing assets are configuration tasks, not runtime broken placeholders. During development, a neutral contact section may state “Contact details will be added before launch.” Public release requires one verified contact method or a conscious owner decision to omit contact. Resume link is absent until a real file is supplied. No form in P0 means no hidden email-sending service.

## 8. Art direction: editorial, precise, personal

The selected direction is an independent strategy journal: warm paper background, deep ink, expressive serif headlines, restrained sans-serif reading text, generous margins, and evidence-led visuals. The personality comes from writing, typography, and project-specific material. The visual system must not imitate a generic AI SaaS landing page.

Global visual references have different jobs: Pentagram for project presentation and grid discipline; The Pudding for interactions integrated into a narrative; Stripe's annual letter for financial storytelling; Linear's UI redesign for hierarchy and component craft; Datawrapper for chart clarity. These are references to study, not a request to copy their layouts, logos, copy, or images.

Named exclusions for visual QA: no purple-to-blue gradient hero, glowing orb, floating glass cards, dot-grid wallpaper, 3D stock robot, endless rounded bento layout, centered hero with indistinguishable CTA pair, marquee of borrowed company logos, scroll hijacking, or decorative chart with invented performance. Subtle motion and occasional asymmetry are welcome when they serve comprehension.

Shadows are absent by default, reserved for genuine overlays. Buttons have at least 44 px interactive height and a visible keyboard focus outline distinct from hover.

Motion: 140–220 ms for controls and modest figure reveals; transform/opacity only unless a layout change genuinely requires it. Content is present before animation. Disable nonessential motion for prefers-reduced-motion. No parallax, typewriter copy, mouse-following cursor, or interaction required to reveal essential text.

Accessibility target WCAG 2.2 AA: semantic landmarks; one H1 per page; logical heading hierarchy; skip link; 4.5:1 body contrast and applicable large-text/UI contrast; meaningful link names; labeled mobile navigation; focus management on close; reduced motion; no hover-only content; alt text and data alternatives. Performance targets for the published site: LCP ≤2.5 s, CLS ≤0.1, and INP ≤200 ms when field data becomes available. Before field data, report Lighthouse/mobile lab results and a documented interaction check without claiming measured field INP. Keep homepage client JS target under 120 KB gzip excluding shared framework overhead; lazy-load application previews and below-fold media. Set image dimensions to prevent shift and avoid loading all demo applications on the homepage.

## 14. Visual quality gate

Before applying the design throughout the site, create the homepage and one case-study vertical slice using realistic content. Inspect at 360, 390, 768, and 1440 px. Refine line breaks, spacing, figure crops, and navigation before propagating components. This is a self-review stage, not an automatic permission pause.

Acceptance questions: Is the name and strategic focus clear without scrolling? Does the site have a distinctive editorial hierarchy? Are all three project previews visually specific to their work? Are long headings intentional on mobile? Can a visitor distinguish evidence from an assumption? Does every primary control do something real? Is the typography still good with fallback fonts and 200% zoom? Is there any unsupported personal or performance claim?

Reject the build if it merely restyles a generic SaaS template, hides weak content behind animations, uses identical placeholder charts for the projects, or requires JavaScript to read the case. Do not accept “looks premium” as validation. Deliver screenshots with a short explanation of the design choices and any remaining asset limitations.

## 15. Analytics, privacy, deployment, and maintenance

P0 works without analytics. If added later, collect page views, project-open, demo-open, and verified resume/contact clicks without recording private model inputs or visitor text. Do not implement session replay by default. Keep analytics opt-in/configuration consistent with the host and applicable requirements.

Generate metadata, Open Graph images from original text/graphics, robots policy, sitemap, and a useful 404 page. Canonical URLs become active only once the real site origin is configured; never publish example.com as the canonical. Draft cases may be noindexed until the owner publishes them. Preview deployments must not imply public launch.

Use the deployment platform already present. If .openai/hosting.json exists, follow Sites instructions. Prepare a reviewable preview and obey the environment's publishing controls. A custom domain, paid services, and public exposure of previously private assets require their appropriate authorization; do not make purchases or invent DNS records. Avoid claiming the site is hosted until verified.

Provide README instructions for editing biography, adding verified links, replacing concept previews, updating project status, adding cases, and running checks. Content validator catches missing source IDs, duplicate slugs, invalid dates, absent assets, and demo actions without URLs. Include an asset-license inventory and a simple handover checklist.
